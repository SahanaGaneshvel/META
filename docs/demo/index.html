Chapter 01 · How a site uses it

You give it your meter data. It tells you how many chargers fit, then runs them.

You give

  • Your building's meter dataone year, downloaded from the utility (a Green Button file)
  • Your utility tariffthe PDF; Muse Spark reads it and checks it reproduces your bill
  • Your grid connection size and chargersfor example 1,000 kW, and how many chargers you want
  • Questions, any timeby text or voice: "6 vans need 40 kWh by 6 am. Can we do it?"

Headroom does

  • Simulates real drivers at your buildingmonths of charging, 15 minutes at a time, before you buy anything
  • Plans every 15 minutes, once installedforecasts the building, charges in the room it leaves
  • Guarantees the limita safety layer nothing can override
  • Sends each charger its powerover OCPP, which commercial chargers already speak

You get

  • How many chargers fit"your connection fits , not ", with the monthly cost
  • Every driver fully charged of the energy they asked for, times over the limit
  • A lower bill lower demand charges than standard smart charging
  • Checked answers"Yes: 240 kWh needed, 409 kWh deliverable without a new peak."

Chapter 03 · The problem

Chargers that can't see the building pile onto its peak.

Standard smart charging splits a fixed charger budget between the cars plugged in. It never sees the building, so on a hot evening it adds its load right on top of the building's peak, the 15 minutes the utility bills.

Chapter 07 · In the real world

A small box on site, the chargers a site already has, and an AI to talk to.

Headroom runs on a small computer next to the building's meter. It reads the meter, plans, and sends each charger its limit over OCPP, the open protocol commercial chargers already speak. The AI sits beside the control loop, never inside it.

Teal: the control loop, which runs on site every 15 minutes with no AI and no internet needed. Indigo: Meta's models, which read documents and talk to people. Every limit a charger receives expires after 15 minutes, so if anything fails the chargers fall back to the installer's safe default.

1

Check

Upload a year of meter data, such as the utility's Green Button export. Headroom Check simulates real drivers on it and says how many chargers fit. No hardware, no commitment.

built · Headroom Check
2

Shadow

Connect the live meter and the charger network. Headroom plans alongside the existing system for a few weeks and reports what it would have done and saved.

next · pilot site
3

Control

Headroom sends each charger its limit over OCPP. The safety layer runs locally and every limit expires, so control fails safe.

4

Expand

Install the extra chargers the check said fit, on the same connection, instead of waiting for a grid upgrade.

Who uses it

  • Owners of offices, malls and campuses adding chargers
  • Charge-point operators running many sites
  • Fleet depots charging vans overnight

Why they pay

  • Delay or avoid a costly service upgrade
  • Lower demand charges on the same energy
  • More chargers, so more drivers served and more charging revenue

What it needs

  • A meter reading every 15 minutes
  • Chargers that speak OCPP, as most commercial chargers do
  • A small on-site computer; a GPU only for offline AI

Already shown

  • Months of real recorded data, held out from tuning
  • 49 more real buildings, and a second utility's tariff
  • Charger commands over real OCPP messages, accepted 480 of 480
  • Meta's models reading real tariffs and answering with checked numbers

Still to prove

  • A pilot on physical chargers at a real site
  • Live meter feeds from utility data services
  • More utilities' tariffs, OCPP 2.0.1, batteries and solar
